# v3.12.0

Your scale can now tune its own NFC readers: put a spool with a TigerTag on the
platform, open **Settings > RFID** and press **Auto**. And if one of the two
readers ever failed to answer at startup, the scale no longer gives up on it for
the rest of its life -- it keeps checking, quietly, and brings it back the moment
it reads a tag.

## Added

- **Auto-calibration of the reader power (Settings > RFID > Auto).** With a
  tagged spool in place it tries every power level and keeps the one that works
  best for the *weaker* of the two readers -- not the one that reads the most
  overall. The two antennas face each other 75 mm apart, so turning the power up
  helps one side and silences the other; picking the highest total would leave
  the scale seeing only half a spool. It takes about half a minute and checks its
  own answer on the best candidates before storing it.
- **The power range goes from 5 steps to 16**, from no drive at all up to the
  reader chip's own factory values, so there is somewhere sensible to land
  between the old steps.

## Fixed

- **A scale that started with only one working reader stayed that way.** The
  startup check asked each reader for its firmware version, and a reader that
  stayed silent once was written off permanently -- including across every later
  restart, with nothing on screen to say why. Owners had to find the setting and
  switch the second reader back on by hand. A reader that does not answer the
  startup check is now simply unconfirmed: the scale keeps checking it in the
  background and puts it straight back to work as soon as it reads a tag.
- **A reader that failed that check was also left unconfigured**, running at the
  chip's factory maximum power for the whole session, which is the worst setting
  when two antennas face each other. The power level you choose now reaches both
  readers whatever the startup check said.
- Several smaller reliability fixes in how the readers are brought up and talked
  to, and a diagnostic message that used to report a configuration failure that
  had not actually happened.

## Known issues

- On one bench unit, the right-hand reader reads 7-byte tags (NTAG / TigerTag)
  normally but cannot read 4-byte MIFARE Classic cards. The fault follows the
  reader, not the card. This is under investigation and is not believed to
  affect TigerTag spools, which use 7-byte tags.

## Updating

Over the air from **Settings > Update**, or over USB with
`bash scripts/flash.sh --fs`. Both keep your WiFi credentials, TigerTag session
and calibration.
